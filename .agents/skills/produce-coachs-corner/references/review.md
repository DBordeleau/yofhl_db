# Editorial review

The editor receives the bible, annotated pilot, approved continuity, the episode's screenplay, brief, facts and pronunciation inventory. Read the material directly; the writer's summary is not a substitute. A fresh subagent should review when available. If one agent must do both jobs, state that limitation in the review.

## Evidence to return

Write findings in `review.md` using screenplay line numbers and short anchors. For each substantial issue, identify what the character is trying to do, how the written exchange deviates, and a specific repair. Read each proposed repair in context before recommending it.

| Dimension | Review question |
| --- | --- |
| Don's convictions | Does the grievance grow from his hockey values or a particular current event? Is there something he sincerely likes? |
| Don's voice | Do fragments, repetition and defensiveness sound motivated? Are quieter lines doing some work? |
| Don's lapses | Can the audience follow what he lost or abruptly returned to? Does Ron plausibly repair it? |
| Ron's function | Is he trying to inform, redirect or finish the broadcast? Has he become a competing insult comic or a lecturer? |
| Relationship | Do corrections, interruptions and reactions show familiarity? Does Ron regain control when needed? |
| Names | Is every named player inventoried with verified birthplace? Does Don use the recorded wrong form for every player born outside North America? Do Ron and cards keep the correct names? |
| Weekly specificity | Could these exact jokes have been written without this week's facts? Which recent jokes are being repeated without development? |
| Arc and timing | Does the opening start an argument, the middle develop it and the ending close something? Are pauses and graphics necessary to the scene? |
| Evidence | Do claims and cards match the same snapshot and scoring period? Are deliberate Don exaggerations distinguished from errors? |

Use the pilot as the comparison point for each weak dimension, with an actual exchange as evidence. Avoid a self-certified overall score such as "9/10, excellent." Identify the weakest scene and revise it before declaring the draft ready. A count of corrections, swears, shouts or callbacks cannot establish quality.

For Don's longer advice passages, identify the point where Ron changes the direction of the exchange. Repeated maxims and a closing explanation of the episode's theme made the October 18 rehearsal sound prepared, even with a plausible Don/Ron word balance. Keep the useful hockey observation, then let a correction, interruption or contradiction put Don under pressure. Record the scene-specific repair; this is not a quota on turn length.

## Factual pass

Read every numeric graphic and factual Ron line against the packet or dated additional evidence. Check roster ownership, games played versus player games, points versus fantasy points, active matchups versus final results, and historical draft facts versus the current week. Confirm `coachs:stats` has captured every player/gap graphic's relevant archived YOFHL season and recorded its provenance in `facts.json` and `brief.md`. Recalculate comparison gaps from the frozen FPts totals; all participants must use the same season. Check that "last year" means the season before the episode, not the latest available row today. Missing records need a documented lookup and an honest fallback, not an unexplained blank card. A missing value remains unknown; rounded FP/G cannot establish exact games played.

Don can deliberately misread or exaggerate a fact, but the writer must identify the intended distortion and the audience must have enough context to understand it. Preserve accurate numbers in the monitor and factual corrections. Never use a fictional group-chat joke as proof of a real owner's conduct.

## Playback pass

Report the actual compiled runtime and Don/Ron word balance as diagnostics. Watch at normal speed, including the bumper and ending. Inspect text overflow, reaction timing, unnecessary long holds, camera changes, mouth pairs and new sound onsets. Check pause, seek and mute when audio or cue code changed. Listen for masking or abrasive levels. Recheck the passages altered after review.

Check monitor cuts against the words they illustrate. An inline graphic at the end of a score-reading line appears only after that score is spoken, and can linger while the next matchup begins. Put each roundup card at its introduction, then verify there is enough reading time. Long speaking passages generally need a talking mouth pair; inspect single-image poses such as `lean` in playback before holding them through a speech.

For a new physical beat, review [preparation, contact and sound](production.md#physical-actions) together. Check whether its intensity matches Don's actual mood and whether Ron's reaction serves the exchange. Observe the effect starting and clearing in motion. Record separately whether the sound was heard, measured or accepted by the owner. The first rehearsal desk sound passed its scan but failed the owner's listening review.

When artwork changed, verify the producer completed the [artwork checklist](../../../../docs/coachs-corner.md#adding-or-replacing-artwork). Inspect fullscreen and narrow layouts in the real player, AVIF and WebP fallback, and mouth transitions with steady clothing and no visible face seams. Record actual quality differences and device/browser coverage in the review.

## Handoff

Finish the review with one clear status: ready for user review, or playable with listed unresolved issues. Record who reviewed, whether the review was independent, what was actually watched/listened to, and what remains unverified. User approval is distinct from this internal status.

Account for each required topic in the owner's notes. Cite its scene or explain the unresolved factual/editorial gap. Keep optional suggestions distinct from required inclusions.

Place proposed new names, predictions, callbacks and persistent style preferences in `continuity-proposals.md` with episode/line evidence. A passing internal review never promotes them to approved continuity. When recording later user feedback, retain both the relevant original passage and accepted revision when that makes the preference clear. Keep the lesson narrow enough that it still permits fresh writing.
